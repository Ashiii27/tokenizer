How LLMs read text

A language model never sees your words. A tokenizer turns them into integers, and an embedding model turns them into vectors that carry meaning. Both run in this tab — no server, no setup, nothing you type leaves your machine.

§ 01 — the vocabulary

Tokens & IDs Xenova/gpt2

A tokenizer cuts text into pieces — whole words, word fragments, single bytes — and maps each piece to an ID from a fixed vocabulary. Those IDs are the only thing the network ever reads. Try Hindi, Chinese or an emoji and watch one character become several tokens.

§ 02 — five vocabularies

            
Tokenizers disagree

            

              There is no universal tokenizer. Each model family learns its own vocabulary, and the
              same sentence costs a different number of tokens depending on which one you use. That
              number is not cosmetic — it is your context budget, your latency and your bill.

§ 04 — meaning as numbers

            

              Vectors & cosine all-MiniLM-L6-v2 · 384d

              similar numbers, so the angle between two vectors — cosine similarity —
              becomes a measure of how close in meaning two texts are.

    § 06 — the shape of meaning
    
            
    The map PCA · 384d → 2d
    
            
    
              Embeddings live in 384 dimensions, which nobody can picture. Project the corpus down to
              two with principal component analysis and semantically similar sentences land near each
              other. Hover a point to read it.
