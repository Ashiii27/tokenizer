§ 00 What a language model actually reads

How LLMs read text

A language model never sees your words. A tokenizer turns them into integers, and an embedding model turns them into vectors that carry meaning. Both run in this tab — no server, no setup, nothing you type leaves your machine.

§ 00 — the guide

Five chapters, one idea each

Every page is a single question, a live demo you can drive, and a short note on what you are looking at. Read them in order — each one assumes the one before it — or jump to what you came for.

  1. 01 Tokens & IDs What the model actually receives. GPT-2 · 1,024-token window →
  2. 02 Tokenizers disagree Five vocabularies, five prices for the same sentence. GPT-2 · Llama 3 · Qwen2.5 · BERT · T5 →
  3. 03 The cost of language Same meaning, several times the tokens. 7 languages measured against English →
  4. 04 Vectors & cosine Meaning as 384 numbers, and what the score hides. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 · 384 dimensions →
  5. 05 A corpus, at once Retrieval: the matrix, the neighbours, the map. up to 40 sentences · PCA to 2D →

What it costs you: the tokenizer chapters download about 1 MB of vocabulary files. The vectors and corpus chapters download a 23 MB embedding model, once, then keep it in your browser's cache. No account, no API key, and nothing you type is ever uploaded.

◆

Everything here runs locally.

Your text is never uploaded. Each model is downloaded once, then served from your browser's cache. Built with Transformers.js, GPT-2's tokenizer and all-MiniLM-L6-v2, on ONNX Runtime over WebAssembly / WebGPU.
