§ 02 — five vocabularies

Tokenizers disagree

There is no universal tokenizer. Each model family learns its own vocabulary, so the same sentence costs a different number of tokens — and that number is your context budget, your latency and your bill.

Try this

  1. Compare one long word: Antidisestablishmentarianism. Count the pieces each vocabulary needs.
  2. Read the multiplier between the columns — the same text can cost twice as much on one model as another.
  3. Look for hollow chips: [CLS] and [SEP] are inserted by BERT's tokenizer, not typed by you.

special token the tokenizer inserts ([CLS], [SEP], <s>) — not in your text byte fragment

What you’re looking at

  • A larger vocabulary means shorter token sequences but a larger embedding table. That trade-off is a design decision, not a defect.
  • Byte-level vocabularies — GPT-2, Llama 3, Qwen — fall back to raw bytes for anything unusual, so text is never unrepresentable.
  • Special tokens occupy context even though you never type them, and they are drawn hollow so they cannot hide inside your sentence.
