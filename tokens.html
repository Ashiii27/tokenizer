§ 01 — the vocabulary

Tokens & IDs Xenova/gpt2

A tokenizer cuts text into pieces — whole words, word fragments, single bytes — and gives each piece an ID from a fixed vocabulary. Those IDs are the only thing the network ever reads.

Try this

  1. Press English, then Hindi + emoji. One Devanagari character often costs two or three tokens.
  2. Click any chip to see its ID, its byte length and where it sits in your text.
  3. Tokenize dog and " dog" — the leading space is part of the token, so the IDs differ.

What you’re looking at

        
    
          
  • Chips are contiguous slices of one decode. Joined back together they reproduce your input exactly — Devanagari, Han characters and four-byte emoji included.
    • 
          
  • A dashed chip is a byte fragment: half a character, completed by the next token. That is why the round-trip check is done on bytes rather than characters.
    • 
          
  • Whitespace is drawn, never hidden: · is a space and ⏎ a newline. A bare space really is its own token.
    • 
          
  • The meter counts against GPT-2's 1,024-token context window — the number that decides how much text fits in one prompt.
